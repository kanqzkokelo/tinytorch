## Summary: "is everything fixable?"

**Yes** — every item above is bounded, scoped, and either has:
- a measured prototype proving the approach (C1, C2, C3, D1, D2, E1, F2)
- a documented spec with file:line hook points (E3, E4, H1)
- a known one-line fix waiting for file free (A1-A3, B1, B4, H1-tail)

**Time estimate** (solo dev, sequential after kernels/qwen2_cuda.cu frees):
- A1-A3: 1-4 hours (iterative bisect)
- B1: 5 minutes (one-line buffer grow)
- C1: 1-2 hours (wire batched prefill)
- C2: 1-2 hours (wire V2 fused PLE)
- C3: 1-2 hours (wire split-K flash)
- C4: 2-3 days (mmap-as-device spike)
- C6: 3-5 days (MMQ port for ≥0.6B models)
- D1: 0.5-1 day (spec-decode wire-in)
- E1: 3-4 weeks (Vulkan P0-P3, kill criterion early)
- E2: 0.5-1 day (K-quant AVX2, in flight)
- E3: 2-3 days (hybrid offload impl — E4B unlock)
- E4: 1 week (server P1+P2 batching)
- F1: ongoing per-family (1-2 hours each: register + gate)
- F4: 2-3 days total (SWA cap → fp16 → q8_0)
- H1-tail: 0.5-1 day (remaining 5 robustness fixes)

**Total: 3-4 weeks of focused work** to close everything except M11 Vulkan (which is a 3-4 week sub-project on its own). The "is everything fixable" answer is *yes* — but not in a single day, and not without a CUDA-capable box.

**Two near-term cheap wins** that would dramatically change the project's positioning:
1. **A1 → m84 green**: 1-4 hours → "5 model families including gemma-4 verified parity-true" becomes provable
2. **C4 → mmap-as-device**: 2-3 days → first genuine "we beat llama.cpp at startup time" claim

Both are doable. C4 has the bigger long-term payoff.
